## The Core Design Tension

Pure stat upgrades (faster vehicles, more damage) are satisfying but forgettable. The most memorable research unlocks **new verbs** — things you can do that you couldn't before. The best research tree has both, but the new verbs are what make factions feel distinct and sessions feel different.

## The Randomness Feel

Your instinct here is really good. Research that feels like discovery rather than a progress bar is much more appropriate to the game's tone. The difference between:

```
"University research: 847/1000 points → Vehicle Speed Tier 2"
```
vs
```
"Your researchers made a breakthrough — vehicles can now be fitted 
 with improved drive systems"
```

The second feels like something happened in your civilization. The first feels like a loading bar.

The probabilistic model fits this perfectly:

```cpp
// Each research cycle (every 10 minutes game time)
void TickCityResearch(ACityActor* City)
{
    int32 UniversityCount = City->GetBuildingCount(EBuildingType::University);
    
    // Base discovery chance + university bonus
    float DiscoveryChance = 0.05f + (UniversityCount * 0.08f);
    
    // Company funding accelerates odds significantly
    if (City->HasFundedCompany(ECompanyType::Research))
        DiscoveryChance *= 2.5f;
    
    if (FMath::FRand() < DiscoveryChance)
    {
        FResearchDiscovery Discovery = PickWeightedDiscovery(City);
        ApplyDiscovery(City, Discovery);
        NotifyPlayer(Discovery);
    }
}
```

The player knows more universities means better odds, but not exactly when something will arrive. That uncertainty makes discoveries feel like events rather than purchases.

    // Pick randomly from weighted pool

## How Privatization Works

When you privatize a lab you're essentially inviting a company in:

```
Player action:    Privatize lab → choose from available companies
                  (companies that exist are influenced by what's happened in your game —
                  kaiju encounters spawn kaiju-related companies,
                  volcanic research spawns materials companies, etc.)

Company moves in: Lab now generates discoveries faster in company's domain
                  Company influence starts at 0, grows each discovery cycle
                  
Influence tiers:
  0-30:   Company is cooperative, does what you ask
  30-60:  Company has preferences, occasionally declines requests
  60-85:  Company shares some discoveries with highest bidder
  85+:    Company is effectively independent, you're just their landlord
```

At high influence you can't simply kick them out either — that costs significant disposition with other companies watching, and the company might relocate to a rival city taking their accumulated research with them.

---

## Nationalizing vs Privatizing As A Spectrum

Rather than a binary switch, it could exist on a spectrum:

```
Fully nationalized → Majority state → Public-private → Majority private → Fully privatized
```

Each step trades control for speed. A player who needs a quick military breakthrough might push a lab toward fully privatized knowing they'll lose control eventually. A player playing long game keeps labs nationalized and accepts slower progress.

You could even allow partial privatization — the company gets 60% of discoveries, you get 40%, influence grows slower. A compromise position with compromise outcomes.
